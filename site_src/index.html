A launch joins this list on its own: every six hours Launch reads the launches GCAT has listed (through Orbit), and those Launch Library 2 reports as flown join sooner, marked provisional, until GCAT lists them. Scheduled launches (the next 14 days) show when you tick them under Show. Chinese launches carry the notices NotamForChina lists for them (number, FIR, window and charted area) from 2012. From December 2020, launches also carry the NOTAM texts Joey0609/notams keeps (mostly China's and its downrange FIRs'). Besides orbital launches the list holds GCAT's suborbital launches and missile firings since 2012, each labelled by kind: sounding rockets and other suborbital rockets, crewed suborbital flights, atmospheric tests, missile tests (lofted ones apart) and missiles fired in combat. How a flight path is estimated: from the bearing to the launch's closure areas; else from the payloads' inclination (GCAT), the orbit's usual inclination, or the last launch of the same programme from the same site, with cos i = cos(latitude) × sin(azimuth) and the direction (north- or south-going) from the site's closure history; else the site's most-used corridor. It is a great circle from the pad, drawn to the farthest closure area or 1,500 NM; not a ground track, and it ignores dog-legs. A suborbital launch or missile has no orbit to infer from: its path is drawn only as charted or towards its farthest closure area. Mission type is Launch's reading of payload names; series the operator describes otherwise but public analysis assesses as military (Yaogan, TJS) say so.

Paste a NOTAM

Paste one or more ICAO-format NOTAMs and they are read and drawn on the map above, in your browser; nothing you paste leaves this page. Coordinates that fit no known form are listed, not guessed. A notice you save goes into the History tab in this browser only.

Saved in this browser

Saved notices appear on the History tab: on the launch they belong to (the same NOTAM on its notices, or a launch inside the notice's window, two hours either side, with its pad within 5,000 NM of the area), else under Show: Notices with no identified launch. They stay in this browser's storage only; nobody else sees them, and clearing site data or a private window loses them, so export them to keep them.

    Trajectory from a pad

    Spherical Earth. The azimuth from the pad to each area's centre stands in for the launch azimuth, and the inclination is what a direct ascent on it gives (cos i = cos latitude × sin azimuth), leaving out Earth's rotation and any dog-leg: indicative, not a launch's orbit. The same inclination is also reached on the mirrored azimuth (180° minus the azimuth), the other branch from the same pad. Dog-leg test: the areas should lie on the great circle from the pad through the farthest area; an area more than 25 NM outside it, or behind the pad, is flagged (a dog-leg, or areas from different events).

    Sources and method

    Feed method launch-1.0. History: GCAT's launch list since 2012 (J. McDowell, planet4589.org/space/gcat, CC BY 4.0): orbital launches kept current through Orbit, and suborbital launches and missile firings (GCAT launch codes S, Y, A, M and L) from GCAT's own tables; Launch Library 2's schedule through Orbit; Launch's charted notices; Chinese launches 2012-2026 and the NOTAMs that closed their drop zones from NotamForChina (credit above). Where Launch's charted notices and NotamForChina hold the same NOTAM (number and FIR), it is shown once, with the charted text, and NotamForChina's area is a check on the charted one. Launch-related NOTAMs with their text from Joey0609/notams (credit above) join a launch by the same NOTAM (number and FIR) where the charted notices or NotamForChina hold it, else by window and place: a GCAT launch of the notice's state inside its window (2 h either side) with its pad within 5,000 NM of the area; the rest are counted, not listed. FAA space-operations TFRs and NGA launch, debris and reentry warnings read from the Internet Archive's copies of tfr.faa.gov and msi.nga.mil join a GCAT launch the same way (a TFR within 100 NM of the pad and a window of at most 7 days); each links to its archived copy. The NASASpaceflight forum's "Chinese NOTAMs thread" and Russian launch discussion are linked from history entries, and Chinese launches link their forum threads from NotamForChina's list of them; the forum itself is not read or collected. Notes on the source map that are not public notices are not shown.
